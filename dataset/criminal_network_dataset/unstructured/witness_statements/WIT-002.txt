WITNESS STATEMENT

Case: FIR-2024-003 (Hawala Operation)
Date of Statement: 12th March 2024
Recorded by: Inspector Singh, Sadar Bazaar PS
Witness: Shop Assistant at neighboring shop, Chandni Chowk, Delhi (Name withheld)

STATEMENT:

I work at the dry fruits shop next to Shop No. 78 in Chandni Chowk. Mohammed Farooq has been running that shop for about 3 years - supposed to be wholesale dry fruits and spices.

But I never see any real customers buying dry fruits there. The shop is always "closed for stock" or "family function" but the shutter is half-open and people go in and out all day.

The people who visit are not normal customers. They are mostly men, well-dressed but not traders. They carry bags - sometimes backpacks, sometimes briefcases. They go in, stay 20-40 minutes, come out without the bags or with different bags.

I recognize some regular visitors:
1. A tall Sikh gentleman with a turban - comes in a Mahindra Scorpio (HR-26-DK-9876). He comes about twice a week. Farooq calls him "Ravi bhai" or "Baldev ji".
2. A Gujarati-looking man - comes in a Hyundai Creta (MH-02-AB-9012). Comes once a week. Farooq calls him "Amit bhai".
3. A lady - comes in a taxi or sometimes a Mercedes. Comes once every few weeks. Farooq calls her "Priya ji" or "Madam". She always wears good clothes, carries a laptop bag.
4. An older man with a beard - comes in a Toyota Innova (DL-1C-MN-4567). Comes when the Scorpio man comes.

Farooq is on the phone constantly. I hear him talking in Hindi, Punjabi, and sometimes what sounds like Arabic or Urdu. He writes in a ledger all the time - not normal shop accounts, looks like codes.

On 10th February, I saw the lady (Priya) come in around 2 PM. She stayed almost an hour. When she left, Farooq walked her to her taxi and they talked quietly for 5 minutes. He looked worried.

The Scorpio and Innova are often parked outside late at night. I've seen boxes being loaded into them after 10 PM.

I never see actual dry fruits being sold or delivered. The "stock" boxes in the shop look empty or have false bottoms.

That's what I've observed in 3 years working next door.
